## Social Proximity and Loan Terms: Evidence from Informal Credit Markets in Rural India (forthcoming)

#### :raising_hand: A. Natal, D. Girollet.

#### :newspaper: *Journal of Development Studies*.

* How does social proximity between borrowers and lenders influence informal lending terms in rural areas of the Global South? We answer this question using a unique dataset collected in 2020-2021 in rural South India. We examine four dimensions of loan agreements: interest rates, collateral, services provided by lenders and borrowers. We analyse the role of social proximity (i.e., homophily and strength of ties) in determining credit terms using loan-level probit models. We find two key results. First, social proximity operates as an ambivalent resource: while homophily and strong ties reduce the financial cost of borrowing, they are also associated with reciprocal obligations. Second, the benefits of social proximity are unequally distributed across social groups. These findings suggest that informal credit markets are deeply embedded in local hierarchies and that policies addressing household indebtedness must go beyond financial inclusion to tackle the structural inequalities shaping credit relations.

#### :arrow_down: Contents
